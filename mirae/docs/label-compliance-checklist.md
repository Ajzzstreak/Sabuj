# Label & Claims Compliance Checklist

Not legal advice. **One-time regulatory consultant review before printing: budgeted at $600 (UNVERIFIED).** It's the cheapest insurance in the plan.

## Decision 1: Is MIRAE Glow a conventional food or a dietary supplement?
This decision sets which claims are allowed, which panel goes on the label, and how Amazon treats the listing.

| | **Conventional food** (Nutrition Facts) | **Dietary supplement** (Supplement Facts) |
|---|---|---|
| Fits when | Represented as coffee: the name "coffee", consumed for taste and routine | Represented as a supplement in powder form, labeled as such |
| Structure/function claims | Allowed **only for effects derived from nutritive value** (e.g. vitamin C's role in immune function or collagen formation). No FDA notification, no disclaimer required | Broader claims allowed (e.g. "supports skin hydration"). **30-day FDA notification** and the **disclaimer** are required |
| Collagen "skin" claims | **High risk.** Not clearly a nutritive-value effect | Allowed with substantiation, notification and disclaimer |
| Amazon | Grocery category (referral 8% ≤$15 / 15% >$15) | Dietary supplement category: **third-party TIC verification (NSF/UL/Eurofins)** before listing |

**FDA's 2014 guidance:**
- Powders labeled as supplements generally aren't treated as beverages, *unless otherwise represented as conventional foods*.
- Names like "drink" and "coffee", and taste-driven use, point toward conventional food.

**Recommendation:** adopt the **same classification your chosen supplier already uses for its existing compliant formula**, and have the consultant confirm it.

If it's a food, launch with:
- vitamin-C-based claims only;
- factual ingredient amounts (e.g. "5 g collagen peptides per stick");
- **no** skin-efficacy claims.

## Allowed vs disallowed claims
| ✅ Generally acceptable (confirm with consultant) | ❌ Do not use |
|---|---|
| "Vitamin C helps the body form collagen" (nutritive role) | "Prevents wrinkles / aging", "reverses skin damage" |
| "Supports immune function" (vitamin C; nutritive) | "Boosts immunity against colds/flu", any disease name |
| "5 g hydrolyzed collagen peptides per stick" (factual) | "FDA approved", "FDA registered product" |
| "Third-party tested" (only with a current lab report) | "100% vitamin delivery", "no nutrient loss" |
| *Supplement route only:* "Supports skin hydration*" + disclaimer | "Replaces your multivitamin" unless the formula actually matches a multivitamin's DVs |
| "Made with Korean-inspired wellness rituals" | "Korean" origin claims if it isn't made in Korea (country of origin must be truthful) |
| | "Ozempic/GLP-1 companion" (the uploaded deck's idea): implied drug or disease association |
| | "Sugar free"/"No sugar" unless < 0.5 g sugars per serving |

**Disclaimer (supplement route), placed next to each claim or linked by asterisk, boxed, bold, ≥1/16 inch:**
*"These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."* Check the exact text against 21 CFR 101.93 before print.

**Biotin:** keep the dose modest (around 100% DV, not mega-dose). FDA has warned since 2017/2019 that high-dose biotin can falsely alter lab tests, including troponin. Consider a line like *"Contains biotin; tell your doctor before lab tests."*

## Required label elements
| Element | Food | Supplement |
|---|---|---|
| Statement of identity (e.g. "Instant Coffee with Collagen & Vitamin C") | ✅ | ✅ (must include "dietary supplement" or a variant) |
| Net quantity (sticks × g; metric + US units, bottom 30% of front panel) | ✅ | ✅ |
| Nutrition Facts **or** Supplement Facts panel | Nutrition Facts | Supplement Facts (21 CFR 101.36) |
| Ingredient list, descending by weight | ✅ | ✅ (other ingredients) |
| Allergen declaration (e.g. "Contains: Fish" if collagen is marine) | ✅ | ✅ |
| Name and place of business of manufacturer/packer/distributor ("Distributed by MIRAE LLC, Queens, NY") | ✅ | ✅ |
| Country of origin (if imported) | ✅ | ✅ |
| Caffeine amount (best practice; expected by consumers) | ✅ | ✅ |
| Domestic address or phone for adverse-event reports | — | ✅ |
| Lot number + best-by date | ✅ (practice) | ✅ |
| UPC (GS1) | ✅ | ✅ |

## Amazon listing restrictions
- Supplement route: **TIC verification** (NSF, UL or Eurofins) via Manage Your Compliance. Without it, listings are suppressed. Budget for this before Amazon launch.
- Listing images and bullets must match the physical label exactly. Claims allowed on the label are the ceiling for the listing.
- No disease claims or before/after skin images in listings.
- Expect expiry-date requirements for consumables. Confirm the current FBA expiration-dated product rules in Seller Central.

## California Prop 65
Coffee's roasting-formed chemicals such as acrylamide fall under the OEHHA coffee exemption (27 CCR §25704, effective Oct 2019), which has been legally challenged. **Added ingredients are not covered.** Ask the supplier for a Prop 65 assessment, especially heavy metals in collagen.

## Pre-print review checklist
- [ ] Classification (food vs supplement) decided and documented by the consultant
- [ ] Panel type matches the classification; %DV maths re-checked (vitamin C DV = 90 mg; 250 mg = 278% DV)
- [ ] Every claim is mapped to substantiation (study or nutritive-role source) in a claims file
- [ ] Supplement route: FDA structure/function notification filed within 30 days of first sale
- [ ] Post-brew vitamin C ≥ label claim (lab report on file), or the claim is lowered
- [ ] Allergen source of collagen (bovine/marine) declared
- [ ] Caffeine per stick stated
- [ ] No "sugar free", "FDA approved" or disease words anywhere (box, stick, inserts, site, ads)
- [ ] Distributor name/address matches the LLC
- [ ] Barcode scans; lot/expiry print area reserved
- [ ] Website, Amazon and ad copy reviewed against the same claims list
